# Whole-theme reading test: pre-registration (reviewer, 2026-10-11, before any result)

Why: Aviyah questioned the first core-reading design: readers were shown a "shared" vs "additional" split, and the
comparator saw only the shared list. Here each theme is read as one coherent whole, and the judge sees both full lists.
Twin/facet labels are uncertain (that is part of the question), so the primary outcome is prevalence against controls,
not agreement with labels.

Sets:
- HIGH: the same 300 high-overlap pairs as the first core-reading pilot (J 0.4-0.7).
- LOW (design check): 60 pairs from the low-overlap sample (20 per J band 0.1-0.2, 0.2-0.3, 0.3-0.4).
- SYNTHETIC VARIANTS (floor): 60 themes A from the HIGH pairs vs A* = a random 70% of A's members (seeded).
  A* is a variant by construction; any "partly/different" here is the procedure's false-positive rate.
Readers (W1): one theme, complete undivided list, no marks; the two themes of a pair, and A vs A*, are read in different
reader sets. Judges (W2): both complete lists with [shared] marks + both stories; all items mixed and shuffled.

Primary outcomes:
1. HIGH re-reading rate (partly or different) minus the synthetic floor, with bootstrap 95% CI.
2. Change vs the old design on the same 300 pairs (old: 23% partly, 0% different).
3. LOW rate minus floor (the design must detect re-reading where the old design did: 39-62%).
Declared reading: the conclusion "high-overlap siblings are mostly variants" is REVISED if HIGH minus floor is >= 15
points above the old design's 23% and its CI excludes 23%; it is CONFIRMED if HIGH minus floor stays <= 30%.
Secondary (labels uncertain): rate by old twin/facet label; AUC vs labels and vs md.
