## 2. The problem (from the user's seat)

An immunologist runs enrichment analysis and gets 200–500 significant pathways per drug target or disease contrast. The list is unreadable because of redundancy from two distinct sources: **across databases** (Reactome, GO, KEGG, BTM describe the same biology under different names) and **within databases** (overlapping gene lists across pathways). So interpretation happens by eye; conclusions are "I feel like there's a metabolism signal"; results aren't comparable across drug targets; and expert hours are burned on a manual, unquantified, irreproducible task. Aviyah did this job by hand at CytoReason — including building the categorization ontology herself, unguided. This spec comes from the user's chair, which none of the academic tools in this space have.

## 3. What the v1 prototype was (archaeology, 20 Aug 2026)

Recovered from `~/code/pathway-tool-old`: 471 immune pathway names (up in RA; BTM et al.) → GPT-4o-mini expansion of each name into a ~75-word functional description → SciBERT embeddings (mean-pooled) → Ward hierarchical clustering → tree construction → LLM cluster labeling → Graphviz render + CSV reports. Three clustering modes were tried: agglomerative, recursive k-means with silhouette-guided splitting, and biologist-guided top-level categories.

**Genuinely ahead of its time:** (1) *expansion before embedding* — MAPA (2025), the strongest paper in the space, does the same and shows it's the biggest quality lever; (2) *abstention in naming* — the labeling prompt allowed "would break cluster into sub-clusters" instead of forcing a name, anticipating the field's 2026 abstention work; (3) *LLM-as-judge miscluster detection* (`LLM_find_misclusterd`) — a validation loop almost no published tool has.

**Weaknesses (the rebuild list):** SciBERT mean-pooled is a known trap (masked-LM, not a sentence embedder); no L2 normalization before Ward; prototype-grade engineering (hardcoded paths, leftover `breakpoint()`, mutable default args, no tests, no packaging, three divergent file copies); no evaluation, no baseline, no quantification layer; clustered on text only, ignoring gene lists.

**Clean-room note:** the `pathwayNamesUpInRA` list may derive from internal CytoReason analysis. The rebuild uses **public data only** — no CytoReason code, data, or internal specifics. Demo inputs are regenerated from public sources (§7).

## 4. Prior art (researched 20 Aug 2026)

The idea became a small subfield after Aviyah prototyped it — validation, not competition. Key entries: **MAPA** (bioRxiv 2025; embeddings of name+description → Louvain modules → RAG-assisted LLM naming; best-evaluated: ARI 0.95 on a hand-curated 44-pathway/12-module ground truth, vs aPEAR/enrichplot 0.33); **SummArIzeR** (Bioinformatics 2026; gene-overlap Jaccard → Walktrap → LLM naming); **EnrichGT** (R, gene-overlap clustering + LLM naming layer); **LLM-PathwayCurator** (bioRxiv 2026; audit-gated abstention); **Hu et al., Nature Methods 2025** (GPT-4 gene-set naming evaluation; the percentile-rank naming protocol); plus the classic non-LLM baselines still in daily use: clusterProfiler::simplify, rrvgo/REVIGO, simplifyEnrichment, Enrichment Map + AutoAnnotate, DAVID, Metascape. QIAGEN IPA ships an LLM "Interpret AI" with zero published validation.

**The field's open weakness:** evaluation. Every tool invents its own tiny, partly circular ground truth; nobody shares a benchmark; naming quality and abstention are barely measured. This is phase 2's opportunity (§10). **The product gap v1 owns:** the quantification layer (§6) — themes as units of analysis with honest statistics — which no published tool provides.

## 5. Architecture decisions (all signed off 20 Aug 2026)

**D1 — The unit of evidence is the gene, not the pathway.** Pathways are overlapping views of the same gene-level evidence; counting pathways double-counts. A theme's gene set is the **union** of its members' genes (deduplication by construction). Quantification = hypergeometric/Fisher test per theme: are the experiment's significant genes over-represented in the theme's gene union vs. the measured background? Consequence: **the tool ingests gene lists, not just names.** Text embeddings for *meaning* (clustering); gene sets for *evidence* (statistics).

**D2 — Global ontology FIRST. [REVERSED 20 Aug 2026 — Aviyah's call, confirmed by the double-dipping analysis.]** v1 builds a **frozen background ontology over all pathways** in the scoped databases (Reactome + GO:BP filtered to 10–500-gene sets + Hallmark + BTM; KEGG excluded for licensing), touching no experiment data — then maps any enrichment result onto it and runs statistics against the fixed theme definitions. Because theme gene unions are constants defined before any experiment, the theme-level hypergeometric test is exactly as valid as standard ORA (the selection circularity is *gone*, not mitigated), and BH-FDR across themes is legitimate again. Required disciplines: (a) ontology version-frozen (built, hashed, dated; stats only ever run against a frozen release); (b) per-experiment adaptations never feed back into tested theme definitions; (c) one declared theme resolution (~30–50 themes, one hierarchy cut) is the tested level. Pathway-*counting* statistics remain descriptive forever (member pathways share genes → correlated significance); inference always runs on gene unions. The ontology itself is a deliverable — a browsable map of pathway space — and is Aviyah's original manual CytoReason ontology assignment, automated. Per-experiment adaptive clustering (fine sub-themes within one result; union-trick comparison) demotes to **v2 as a qualitative/exploratory feature**. Curated descriptions from Reactome/GO mean LLM expansion is only needed for name-only sources (BTM). Scale risk (~10–20k pathways) is a quality risk, not compute: mitigations are the scoped sources, Reactome's curated hierarchy as a built-in QC anchor (our themes should broadly recover its top levels), build-small-first (pipeline validated on a ~500-pathway demo set before scaling), and an explicit Day-3 go/no-go with fallback to a Reactome-only v1 ontology (2,900 pathways) if quality disappoints.

**D3 — Soft membership.** Hard clustering (Ward) *discovers* themes; a second pass *assigns* softly: every pathway scores against every theme centroid; primary = argmax, secondaries above threshold are reported with scores. Biologically honest ("TNF signaling in adipose tissue" is immune *and* metabolic). The v1 miscluster-judge idea returns here: an LLM-judge "doesn't belong" verdict + a close second centroid = dual membership, not error. Gene-level stats are unaffected (a pathway's genes legitimately evidence both themes); descriptive counts flag dual members so nothing silently double-counts.

**D4 — Statistics, honestly caveated. [REVISED 20 Aug 2026 — Aviyah caught the double-dipping problem; comprehensive research (selective-inference + pathway-statistics literatures) confirmed her and overturned the original design.]** Original design (per-theme hypergeometric on the gene union vs. the DEG list, BH-FDR across themes) is **withdrawn as inference**: the theme was assembled from pathways selected for enrichment in those same DEGs, so the test statistic sits in the extreme tail of its null *by construction* (circular analysis, Kriegeskorte 2009); additionally the gene-sampling urn model is invalid under gene–gene correlation (Goeman & Bühlmann 2007), worst for exactly the large co-expressed unions themes produce. BH on invalid p-values controls nothing — FDR requires valid per-test p-values as input. Notably, no published tool solves this; the dominant convention (Metascape/MAPA report the best member p-value as the cluster's) is a winner's-curse minimum and *worse* than refusing.

**[Superseded in part by the D2 reversal, same day: with a priori themes, valid inference returns to v1 — see the two-tier design below. The descriptive layer survives as Tier B and as the effect-size companion in Tier A.]**

**Two input tiers (v1):** **Tier A** — user supplies their DEG list + measured universe → full valid inference: gene-level ORA of the DEG list against each frozen theme union, fold enrichment + intervals, BH-FDR across themes at the declared resolution. **Tier B** — user supplies only an enrichment table → their pathways are mapped onto the ontology; output is the descriptive layer only, clearly labelled, no new p-values. Remaining caveats in Tier A are exactly the industry-standard ORA caveats, stated in the docs: gene co-expression violates urn independence (shared by all ORA tools); measured-universe background required (enforced/surfaced); DEG-threshold dependence.

The descriptive layer (both tiers) quantifies **without fake inference** — which still delivers everything Aviyah originally wanted ("X pathways relate to this, counted not felt"): per theme, (a) gene union size and DEG coverage; (b) **fold enrichment** (observed/expected) with an uncertainty interval, explicitly labelled descriptive — effect-size reporting is recommended practice (PLoS Comp Biol "Ten common mistakes" 2026); (c) the **redundancy index** = 1 − |union|/Σ|member sets|; (d) the **independent-signal count**: a conditional de-nesting pass (PathwayDenester-style modified hypergeometric, computable from the table alone) reporting how many member pathways remain significant after conditioning on overlap with stronger members — "12 pathways, 3 independent signals" is the honest version of the original counting idea; (e) member q-value ranges, transparently. Background/universe handling stays (user-supplied measured universe; Wijesooriya 2022: 95% of published ORA gets this wrong — the tool surfaces it). Inferential upgrades live in phase 2 (§10): MGSA-style joint Bayesian set modeling (posterior activation handles overlap inherently; table-only input), closed-testing FDP bounds ("≥k true discoveries in this theme"; valid under any post hoc grouping), random-gene-list full-pipeline empirical nulls (competitive null, honest about selection), and — when raw data exists — data splitting + camera/roast, or a priori themes from the global ontology. The README states all of this; the statistical honesty is itself the differentiator in a field that decorates clusters with invalid p-values.

**D5 — Core output object fixed now; visualizations chosen from real data on D5 of the sprint.** Per theme: name (abstention allowed), members with membership scores, gene union + coverage, redundancy index, enrichment statistic + FDR, plus an explicit "unclustered" bin — as clean JSON/table any view can build on. Likely-surviving views: interactive tree (the v1 centerpiece, done right) and theme table; comparison view if the union trick lands in scope. Decision deferred deliberately — views get chosen with real output on screen, with Aviyah as the target user.

**D6 — LLM provider: Claude API, behind a provider-agnostic interface.** A minimal `LLMClient` abstraction (complete(prompt) → text, with caching to disk); Claude adapter first, OpenAI adapter trivial to add. All LLM calls (expansion, naming, judge) cached and committed so the demo re-runs free and deterministic.

**D7 — Embeddings: biomedical sentence-embedding model primary, general model as comparison arm.** Primary: BioLORD-2023 (trained so embedding geometry mirrors ontology structure — exactly this task). Comparison: Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (Apache 2.0, strong general model). Both run locally via sentence-transformers in seconds at this scale; both pinnable. **L2-normalize, then Ward on Euclidean** (for unit vectors, ‖a−b‖² = 2−2·cos — Ward becomes legitimate; unnormalized Ward clusters partly by vector magnitude ≈ text length, a silent bug v1 had). "Generic vs. ontology-aware embeddings produce different groupings, here's the ARI between them" is an interview-grade observation from a one-day comparison.

**D8 — Hierarchical testing + leftover decomposition (added 20 Aug 2026, Aviyah's design).** Rather than testing one flat theme resolution, v1 tests down the frozen tree: top-level themes first, **descending only into significant parents** (Meinshausen 2008-style hierarchical testing; TreeBH/Yekutieli as FDR variants — descent-on-significance is the multiplicity control, with a documented α-inheritance scheme). Then a **leftover decomposition** (topGO `elim` idea, refined by Aviyah): siblings never subtract from each other — each child is tested on its full gene set during descent. For the umbrella decision, the leftover = parent's union minus **genes unique to the significant children** (genes a significant child shares with other siblings or parent-level annotations stay in — full removal would wrongly delete a co-significant sibling's shared evidence, e.g. T∩B genes, biasing toward child-only reporting). Leftover enriched → parent carries signal beyond its named children → report parent as umbrella + significant children as detail; not enriched → report significant children only. Attribution under overlapping sets has no uniquely correct rule (≈ correlated covariates in regression): unique-removal (Aviyah's, default) and full-removal (topGO's) are both exposed as parameters; the choice and rationale is `DECISIONS.md` entry #1. **Distinctiveness diagnostic (added same day, Aviyah; revised same day after her power objection):** reporting decisions stay on the child's **full** gene set (the powered test) — a standalone significance test on tiny unique sets was rejected as a power cliff that would wrongly demote distinct children. Distinctiveness is instead a **graded, no-threshold panel** per significant child: (a) Aviyah's ablation delta — enrichment score with vs without the unique genes (algebraically: uniques improve the score iff their DEG rate exceeds the shared core's, k_u/m_u > k/m); (b) fold enrichment of unique genes vs *background* with an uncertainty interval (needed because a very hot shared core can make genuinely enriched uniques look non-contributing under (a) alone); (c) observed vs expected unique-DEG count. Reporting is by **annotation, not suppression**: both T and B report individually when each has unique-gene contribution; a child with no unique signal vs background and shared-driven enrichment is still shown, labelled "driven by genes shared with siblings — see parent," its evidence feeding the umbrella decision. Principle unchanged — evidence attributed to the smallest node containing its ambiguity — implemented as labelling rather than gating (`DECISIONS.md` entry #2). **Rank-based variant (same day, Aviyah — preferred when input allows):** with a full ranked gene list, run per-node GSEA down the tree instead of ORA — removes DEG-threshold dependence AND restores power for small unique sets (rank statistics use every gene's position). Input tiers: **A1** ranked list → GSEA machinery; **A2** DEG list + universe → ORA machinery; **B** table-only → descriptive.

**Literature check on the unique-contribution test (same day):** the with/without construction is the published pattern, but validated methods use it as a **conditional p-value, not a Δ of scores** (ΔNES has no null and shrinks with set size — kept as display only). Adopted precedents: sibling overlap → `fgsea::collapsePathways` / `collapsePathwaysORA` construction (re-test child on universe minus shared genes; de facto standard, maintained reference implementation; known wart: default permutation count too low, use ≥10⁴/multilevel) and SetRank's pairwise A\B / B\A logic (best-benchmarked: 42 datasets vs 16 methods, top specificity); child-given-parent → parent–child hypergeometric (Grossmann 2007: background = parent's gene union); parent-given-children (the leftover test) → topGO elim / GOstats conditional (Alexa 2006; Falcon & Gentleman 2007). Descriptive corroboration in A1: leading-edge Jaccard between child and parent (GSEA standard). Phase 2 cross-check: pareg (2023; regularized regression of gene p-values on the full membership matrix — partial contribution jointly, validated but young) or MGSA. Output = the minimal set of nodes, at the right depths, that carries all the signal — the tool's redundancy thesis applied to its own statistics. Non-circular: the tree is frozen a priori; only the traversal is data-driven, which is what these procedures are designed for. Supersedes D2(c)'s single-declared-resolution rule, which remains the fallback if implementation time runs short.

**D8 — FINAL (21 Aug 2026, supersedes everything above in D8):** the statistics layer converged, through four design iterations driven by Aviyah, to the **three-test protocol** recorded verbatim in the repo's `DECISIONS.md` ("Hierarchical reporting protocol"): (1) BASIS — every node tested on all its genes, **no significance-gated descent** (a diluted parent can hide a real child — Aviyah's catch), one BH family across all basis tests (valid under positive dependence, Benjamini & Yekutieli 2001); the tree is the reporting structure, not a testing gate. (2) CHILD-UNIQUE (attribution) — per significant child, conditional test + effect size on genes in no sibling (collapsePathways/SetRank construction); not contributing → the parent is the finding ("common biology"). (3) PARENT-BEYOND (attribution) — per parent, conditional test + effect size on genes in non-significant children only (GOstats construction); contributing → parent carries more than its enriched children. Attribution p-values get their own per-layer BH (family = unit of interpretation); the selection of the attribution family by basis results is a stated caveat with Benjamini & Bogomolov 2014 (JRSS-B 76:297) as the phase-2 exact upgrade. Effect sizes are mandatory beside every attribution test; attribution never gates basis significance.

**FDR status under dependence (21 Aug 2026, provisional — final decision deferred to implementation):** BH's guarantee is unproven for nested overlapping set tests (PRDS not established) but is field standard and empirically robust; BY offered as a guaranteed-under-any-dependence switch (~ln m power cost); an **empirical-FDR mode** (random matched-size gene lists run through the whole tree, ~10³ draws) calibrates against the tree's exact dependence and serves as an audit of BH — agreement validates BH, disagreement ships as the honest number. Permutation calibration is exact under the complete gene-sampling null; known limits, disclosed: partial-null spillover (null nodes overlapping true signal — interpreted by the attribution layer), realized-vs-expected FDP spread under dependence (Owen 2005), Monte Carlo resolution ~1/draws, and the urn-model/co-expression caveat shared with all ORA. **Definitive end-to-end check = planted-truth simulation** (synthetic DEG lists with known enriched nodes → measure realized FDP and attribution error of the full pipeline) — added to the phase-2 evaluation suite as its first item. The repo's `DECISIONS.md` is authoritative for this protocol.

## 11. The interview story (skeleton, to be finished Day 6–7)

At CytoReason I watched immunologists interpret 400-pathway enrichment lists by eye — including doing the categorization manually myself — so I prototyped an LLM/embedding tool to automate it (2024/25). The field later published the same architecture (MAPA, SummArIzeR, 2025–26), which told me the idea was right — so this year I rebuilt it properly: [what changed and why — wrong embedder fixed, statistics added at the gene level so redundancy dissolves instead of double-counting, soft membership, abstention in naming]. Here's the live demo; here's the decision log; here's what it still gets wrong and what I'd evaluate next [phase 2]. Built hands-on with Claude Code.

**The five questions to have cold (Day 6–7 prep):** (1) *Why not just use GO/Reactome's own hierarchies?* — they're per-database; the redundancy problem is cross-database; native hierarchies are curatorial (is_a / reaction-mechanistic), not interpretation-sized; name-only sources (BTM, Hallmark) have no hierarchy at all; positioning, converged over three corrections (Aviyah caught both the overstatement AND the over-correction, 22 Aug): **THEMA has two distinct edges** — (i) the honest statistics + reporting engine (calibrated FDR, attribution protocol, minimal-node output — absent from ALL existing tooling, GO-only included: topGO's conditional p-values are self-described scores, GO slims are crude static buckets), valuable to every user; (ii) the cross-source merge, unique to THEMA. GO-only users get edge (i) in full; multi-source users get both. **Architectural consequence (Aviyah's push, 22 Aug): the D8 statistics engine is TREE-AGNOSTIC — support a "native hierarchy mode"** where GO's own DAG or Reactome's own tree plugs directly into the engine (no clustering, no recovery risk; THEMA's contribution = pure statistics/reporting layer). Nearly free at Phase 4 (the tree is an input parameter); flagship mode remains the built cross-source ontology. Makes the justification bulletproof from both directions. Supporting evidence: CytoReason's lists were multi-source (btm.* + others) — which is exactly why the work was manual, i.e., why a human had the job; and five competitor tools (MAPA et al.) motivated themselves on the same multi-database pain, post-dating Aviyah's prototype. The reactome2go eval (2b) is this question's empirical test, by design (Aviyah raised it 22 Aug; it's the project's null hypothesis). (2) Why hierarchical clustering / why this attribution protocol? (3) Why is your FDR valid when per-result theming isn't? (4) What breaks it? (5) What next? Also the code-review triage rule, stated: infrastructure verified behaviorally (tests, idempotence, known-count checks); the scientific core (stats engine, cut logic, prompts) read line-by-line — full guided read scheduled at Phase 4.
