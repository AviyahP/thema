# Chain collapse at 0.85 with a support survivor rule. 9 Oct 2026

**EXPLORATORY. The rule FAILS its own declared test: three of the five criteria fail.** The rule is
not adopted, and nothing was changed to make a criterion pass. The frozen baseline stands.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | no containment pair at ratio >= 0.85 remains | **PASS** -- 0 pairs |
| **(b)** | every multi-parent baseline theme keeps all its parents | **FAIL** -- 971 exceptions over 5,183 multi-parent themes |
| **(c)** | no pathway loses all its themes under 1,000 members | **PASS** -- 0 stranded pathways |
| **(d)** | reproducibility at J >= 0.7 higher after the rule | **FAIL** -- 77.5% -> **76.4%** |
| **(e)** | carried plausibility at least 97.7% | **FAIL** -- **97.6%** |

The rule change is **POST-HOC** -- threshold 0.85 and keep-the-higher-support were chosen after
seeing the n00150 lattice and the 550-pair survivor measurement. The five criteria were **declared
before the builds ran** (DECISIONS, 2026-10-09 23:38:04Z) and are reported here unchanged.

## What was built

| | |
|---|---|
| `data/ontology/v0.4-leaves/thema_L_x2_c85` | the baseline's material, rule changed. **8,251 themes** |
| `data/ontology/v0.4-leaves/thema_L_repair_c85` | the independent build, same rule. 8,291 themes |

`data/ontology/frozen/` was not touched; `tests/ontology/test_frozen_baseline.py` still passes.

## 1. The route used

**Re-ran the build from the cached sides with the one changed rule** -- same universe, trees, floors,
thresholds and seeds; only `--collapse-share 0.85 --survivor support` differs.

The alternative, applying 0.85 as a second post-pass over the baseline's exported tables, was
**rejected**: the baseline has already collapsed every pair at or above 0.90 under keep-the-parent,
so a second pass would produce a hybrid of two survivor rules rather than the declared rule.

**The harness is proved, not assumed.** Rebuilding each universe at 0.90 with the old survivor rule
reproduces its build **byte-identically** in all four tables:

| rebuild | `nodes.tsv` | `members.tsv` | `edges.tsv` | `unplaced.tsv` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `thema_L_x2_verify` vs the frozen baseline | identical | identical | identical | identical |
| `thema_L_repair_verify` vs `thema_L_repair` | identical | identical | identical | identical |

The only manifest difference is the new `survivor` key. So the difference between the baseline and
`thema_L_x2_c85` is the rule and nothing else. Both c85 builds were also run twice and came out
byte-identical, so the new rule is deterministic.

Cost: 326 s and 1.4 GB peak for `thema_L_x2_c85`, 321 s and 1.5 GB for `thema_L_repair_c85`, 340 s
and 1.3 GB for the x2 harness check, 332 s for the repair harness check. The report itself is 3 s.

The rule lives in `src/thema/ontology/repair.py` as `collapse_chains(..., survivor=...)`, with
`"parent"` the default so every existing caller and the baseline are unaffected. Nine new tests in
`tests/ontology/test_repair.py` cover it.

## 2. Reproducibility (criterion d) -- FAIL

Projected comparison, excluded keys removed from the other build's sets, as before. Support band is
the band of the theme being counted.

| support band | before: themes | matched | share | after: themes | matched | share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| < 0.1 | 3,784 | 2,361 | 62.4% | 3,490 | 2,088 | **59.8%** |
| 0.1-0.3 | 2,045 | 1,800 | 88.0% | 1,919 | 1,668 | **86.9%** |
| 0.3-0.5 | 996 | 881 | 88.5% | 955 | 837 | **87.6%** |
| >= 0.5 | 1,915 | 1,735 | 90.6% | 1,887 | 1,708 | **90.5%** |
| **OVERALL** | **8,740** | **6,777** | **77.5%** | **8,251** | **6,301** | **76.4%** |

Before is `thema_L_x2` against `thema_L_repair`; after is `thema_L_x2_c85` against
`thema_L_repair_c85`. The before figure reproduces the previously reported **77.5%** exactly, which
is the method's own control.

**Reproducibility goes DOWN, by 1.2 points, and down in every single band.** The criterion asked for
an increase.

**Why, measurably.** The survivor rule is close to a coin flip on a large minority of pairs. Over the
1,147 collapse events the support margin between the two nodes has median 0.120, but **28.3% of
pairs are decided by a margin under 0.05** and 1.7% are exact ties. Two independent builds do not
agree on which node of a near-tied pair has the higher support, so they keep different survivors and
the matched share falls. Keep-the-parent has no such sensitivity: the parent is the parent in both
builds.

## 4. Criterion (b) -- FAIL, and what the 971 exceptions are

971 multi-parent baseline themes lose a parent that was **not** chain-collapsed with another parent
of the same theme, which is the only exception the rule declared.

- **296** of the lost parents still exist in the new build **and are still an ancestor of the theme,
  one level up** -- the facet is still above the theme, just further away.
- **675** of the lost parents are **gone entirely**, absorbed into something else. For those the
  facet is lost as a distinct node.

The exceptions are not confined to near-copies: the lost parent's ratio ranges from 0.009
(`n00017` losing the 1,245-member giant `n03232`) to 0.879, so re-parenting after a collapse detaches
genuine facets, not only staircase rungs.

## 8. What this says

The rule does the local job it was designed for and fails the global test.

- It removes the staircase: 489 fewer themes, all of the loss in the 11-50 band, the n00150 lattice
  cleaned of exactly its two weakest rungs, and no qualifying pair left anywhere.
- It leaves the real problem alone: **121 roots and the same ten giants, unchanged.**
- It costs reproducibility in every support band, because the keep-the-higher-support tie-break is
  decided by a margin under 0.05 on 28.3% of pairs and is therefore not stable across builds.
- It detaches 675 facets outright, and shortens the lattice for four fifths of the universe by a
  median of two levels.

**Three of five criteria fail, so by the declared rule the arm is not better than the baseline and is
not adopted.** The two parts worth separating in any next attempt: the threshold at 0.85 looks
defensible on its own, while **the support survivor rule is the part that costs reproducibility** --
it was never tested separately, and a 0.85 arm with the baseline's keep-the-parent survivor is the
obvious untried combination. That is not a decision; it is the next question.
