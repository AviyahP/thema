# Naive K — a direction probe before the full Arm K run. 7 Oct 2026

**EXPLORATORY, and deliberately naive.** Arm K only: no K-snn, no BioLORD, no transfer, no HiDeF
grid, no E1–E4. **Cutoffs chosen in advance and not fitted** — support ≥ 0.33 and lifetime ≥ 2,
meaning the group survives while *k* at least doubles. One sanity build on scramble seed 3001 with
the same cutoffs.

Branch `engines-2026-10`. The frozen build, the naming code and arms C, G, H are untouched. All code
is in **new files** — `src/thema/ontology/mutualrank.py`, `scripts/naive_k.py`,
`scripts/naive_k_report.py` — so `recurrent.py`, `cut_trees.py` and `engines.py` are unchanged and no
cached side's provenance moves.

**The full Arm K spec and Amendment A1 are ON HOLD, not cancelled**, and both are marked as such in
place. Nothing about them is amended by this probe.

## Early stop: NOT triggered

> The rule: stop if the scramble build gives more than 5% as many themes as the real build.
> **Scramble 3001 gives 0 themes at the declared lifetime ≥ 2, against the real build's 593 —
> 0.00%.** At the report-only cutoff 1.5 it gives 6 against 2,276, which is 0.26%.

So the probe ran to completion.

Share of themes whose best match to a curated set **of the same source** exceeds Jaccard 0.5.

**Naive K's themes match curated biology far better than anything previously built here** — 59.5%
against arm A's 38.6% on Reactome, and 25.8% against 9.0% on GO. On GO it is **29× HiDeF maxres
25's 0.9%**. The proxy is not the protocol's E1 and should not be read as it, but the gap is not
marginal.

**The catch is coverage, and it is severe.** Those 59.5% are 232 themes. The build places 1,410 of
10,770 pathways and leaves **90.6% effectively unplaced**, against arm A's 1.31%. **And there is no
middle at all**: zero themes of 51–200 members at the declared cutoff, and 16 at 1.5.

| all candidates proposed | 49,352 | 28,041 | 18,740 | **2,392** | 177 | 2 |

| + lifetime ≥ 1.5 | 4,164 | 2,794 | 1,284 | **86** | 0 | 0 |
| + lifetime ≥ 2 | 968 | 797 | 171 | **0** | 0 | 0 |

**The engine does propose mid-size structure — 2,569 candidates of 51–500 members.** Support removes
87% of them (their median support is **0.020**), and lifetime removes the rest. The mid-size
survivors of the support gate have median lifetime **1.250**, below the cutoff.

Lifetime ≥ 2 is a high bar for everything, not specifically for the middle: even 3–10 candidates
that pass support have median lifetime **1.333**. On a ladder from 3 to 100 a group must hold
through roughly three consecutive rungs to double *k*, and most do not.

## What the probe says about the direction

**Good, on two of three axes.** The lifetime score works on real data: the scramble goes to exactly
zero while 593 real themes survive, and the surviving themes are markedly better matched to curated
biology than arm A's or HiDeF's. It costs 8.8 seconds.

**Bad on the third, and badly.** At the declared cutoff it places 9.4% of the universe and produces
no theme between 51 and 500 members. Under the engine rule's own gates this would fail G3
(effectively unplaced ≤ 5%) by a factor of eighteen, and it would have nothing to offer in the two
middle bands that the whole engine search exists to fill.

**One finding from the toy tests is worth carrying forward**, because it contradicts the real data
and I would rather it be on record than rediscovered. On a planted fixture — two tight groups of
twelve plus ten noise points — **lifetime is inverted**: the noise triples score 42.67 and the
planted groups 32.0, because `L = (rung after last) / (first rung)` and a tiny isolated triple
connects at the lowest rung, giving it the smallest denominator. On the real 10,770 the inversion
does not appear and the scramble is cleanly separated. The toy confounds size with plantedness, so it
is a warning rather than a verdict — but the warning is that this score's denominator rewards
forming early, and on data where small groups form earliest that could flip the sign. The test is
written to fail loudly if the toy inversion ever stops holding.
