**\*** = the rule-of-three bound decided the cut: no scrambled candidate was observed at or above
it across the ten calibration seeds, so the mean count was replaced by 3/10 rather than read as a
zero rate. **Six of the eight new strata rest on that substitution**, which is the honest way to
report them: their calibration FDR is an upper bound, not an observation.

**A latent limit of the declared rule, worth recording because it bites silently.** A bounded cut's
FDR is `0.3 / kept`, so it only reaches 0.008 once **38 or more** real candidates clear the cut. A
stratum with fewer than that above the scrambles' maximum can never pass on the bound, however clean
the separation looks — it would need more scramble seeds, not a better cut. Every new stratum here
has at least **96**, so none is close to the edge; but a finer stratification would start hitting it.
`tests/test_o50s.py` asserts the 37/38 boundary so it cannot be forgotten.

Three things to read here.

**Every new stratum from 20-39 up lands on the same floor, 0.01** — support 2/200, "found in at
least two subsamples". That is the next rung above 0.005 on the cut ladder, and it is where the
scrambles stop: prompt 41 showed that above 40 pathways no scrambled grouping is ever found twice.
The floors are not tuned; they are the first cut past the noise.

**The 10-19 stratum's floor went *up*, from 0.025126 to 0.030151.** Splitting the pool did not
loosen the small end — it tightened it, because 10-19 is where the null is genuinely noisy
(80 distinct support values, up to 0.170) and it no longer has 86,000 larger candidates diluting its
rate. So the change is not a one-way relaxation.

**The two worst held-out rates are both in the frozen strata**, 7-9 at 0.01088 and 5-5 at 0.01042,
and both are unchanged from O50 because those floors are unchanged. Every new stratum is at or below
0.00613, and six of them are at **0.0**. The overall rate falls to 0.00233.

## 9. What this says

**The diagnosis from prompt 41 was right and the fix works.** Pooling sizes 10 to 2,809 under one
floor was throwing away evidence; splitting it recovers 1,056 themes, nearly triples the 25-99 bands,
grows 100-199 six fold, keeps 118% of the small themes and improves summary placement by 7.3 points
to **92.6%**. The held-out FDR is better than O50's, not worse. All three criteria ccode owns pass.

**Four things a decision has to weigh, and none is in the declared criteria.**

**The new mid-size themes are weakly supported.** 0.010 to 0.020 — two to four subsamples in 200. The
calibration says that is above noise and the held-out rate for those strata is 0.0, but six of the
eight new floors rest on a **rule-of-three upper bound** rather than an observed null, so their
calibration FDR is a bound and not a measurement. More scramble seeds would tighten it; ten is what
the cal8 set has.

**A new 2,096-pathway giant appeared** whose best O50 match is 0.315, and nothing in the declared
plan reads it. It holds 38% of the universe.

**The structure got busier.** Multi-parent 43.7% → 63.3%, busiest node 283 → 382 children, roots
188 → 261. If the ontology is meant to be browsed, that is a cost the placement figure does not
capture.

**O50s and the areas pass disagree.** Only 2 of 59 new large themes match a rec2fix area at 0.7.
Whatever the missing layer is, these two methods do not find the same thing in it, and adopting one
is not evidence for the other.

**Adopted: nothing, by declaration.** O50s is a candidate to replace O50 and the remaining gates are
the reviewer's blind reading, the intruder test, and your call.
