## 17:30:27Z — why, and it is a design flaw rather than a tuning problem

| side | fine kept | units entering level 1 | level sizes | close thresholds per level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **real** | 622 | 8,562 | 1,423 / 264 / 102 / 71 / 37 / 30 | **0.307** / 0.527 / 0.434 / 0.335 / 0.298 / 0.266 |
| 3001 | 4 | 10,762 | 3,653 / 1,564 / 675 / 293 / 134 / 69 | **0.086** / 0.087 / 0.090 / 0.093 / 0.092 / 0.090 |
| 3002 | 5 | 10,760 | 3,656 / 1,566 / 666 / 288 / 134 / 77 | 0.086 / … |
| 3003 | 4 | 10,762 | 3,633 / 1,545 / 668 / 299 / 141 / 66 | 0.086 / … |

Two things combine, and the second is the fault.

**The fine level filters noise and then hands almost nothing on.** On a scramble it keeps 4 themes,
so **10,762 of 10,770 units are single pathways**. The level step therefore runs on raw pathways,
not on themes, and whatever it does next is unconstrained by the fine level's work.

**"Close" is defined as a PERCENTILE, so it admits 1% of pairs no matter what the data is.** On the
real matrix the 99th percentile of pairwise centroid cosine is **0.307**; on a scramble it is
**0.086**. The threshold simply follows the data down. A cosine of 0.086 between two random
centroids is not closeness in any meaningful sense, but it is the 99th percentile of that matrix, so
the near-clique step treats it as though it were — and 1% of 58 million pairs is 580,000 edges,
which is more than enough for greedy near-cliques to find thousands of groups.

**That is the flaw: a relative threshold is scale-free by construction, and scale-free is exactly
what cannot reject a null.** The percentile was chosen so that one setting works across levels whose
units differ in kind, and it does that well — the real build's thresholds move sensibly from 0.307 to
0.527 and back down as units grow. The same property makes the step incapable of saying "there is
nothing here".

The join-share condition does not save it either. Requiring a unit to be close to 80% of a group is a
*shape* condition on the close graph, and a random graph with 580,000 edges has plenty of dense
subgraphs. Tightening the share to 90% would shrink both sides; it would not change which side is
larger.

## What this says, in plain terms

**The fine level is sound and the merge step is not.** KC's one-filtration lifetime is a genuine
noise filter — 4 scrambled candidates against 622 real — and it is the cheapest one here, at 0.9
seconds for the whole fine level. The recursive near-clique step then throws that away by grouping on
a threshold defined relative to whatever matrix it is given.

**The middle KC appeared to build is an artefact of that step**, and the band table is how it shows:
at 51–200 the null produces 91% as many themes as the real data. A middle that a scramble reproduces
is not a middle.

**What would have to change.** An absolute closeness floor, or a null-calibrated one — the same
scramble machinery the floors use, applied to the merge step rather than only to the fine level. That
is a different design and not a parameter of this one, which is why the early stop is the right
outcome rather than a sensitivity sweep. Three settings were declared as sensitivities (q at 97 and
99.5, join share at 70% and 90%, t at 1.5 and 2) and none of them is an absolute threshold, so none
of them addresses this.
