## Step 3's held-out check FAILED, and the declared rule was applied

This is the declared rule running as written, not a post-hoc change.

**The stored v0.4 floors do not confirm on L.** Checked once on L's held-out scramble 4001:

| stratum | held-out FDR | |
|---|---:|---|
| 3-3 | 0.01154 | |
| 4-4 | 0.0 | |

| 6-6 | 0.00283 | |
| 7-9 | 0.0 | |
| 10+ | 0.0 | |
| overall | 0.00332 | inside the 0.01 cap |

The overall figure passes; **stratum 5-5 does not**. So per step 3 the floors were **recalibrated on
L from scrambles 3001–3003** and re-checked on 4001:

| | stored (v0.4) | recalibrated on L |
|---|---|---|
| 3-3 | 0.79 | **0.84** |
| 4-4 | 0.91 | **0.925** |
| 5-5 | 0.368687 | **0.425** |
| 6-6 / 7-9 / 10+ | 0.33 | 0.33 |
| overall held-out FDR | 0.00332 | **0.00241** |
| worst stratum | 0.02715 (5-5) | **0.0297 (5-5)** |

**The recalibration did not clear the cap.** 5-5 moved from 0.02715 to 0.0297 — marginally worse,
despite its floor rising from 0.369 to 0.425. The mechanism is visible in the counts: on the
held-out side, stratum 5-5 has **6 null discoveries against 202 real ones**, and raising the floor
removed 19 real discoveries and *none* of the 6 nulls. Those nulls sit well above both floors. The
stratum is also where scrambling produces the most spurious structure on L — 8,360 null candidates
against the real side's 1,455.

`solve()` sets each floor so the stratum's **calibration** FDR is ≤ 0.01, and 5-5 achieves 0.0099
there. The cap is breached only on the single held-out seed, which is a small-count problem: one
extra null discovery in that stratum moves its FDR by about 0.005.

**Floors of 0.5 and above would pass** — the trade-off is recorded in
`data/experiments/leaves/stratum_5-5_floor_tradeoff.txt` — **and none was applied.** Choosing a
floor because the held-out side says it passes is fitting to the held-out side, which is the one
thing it exists to prevent. The declared rule's remedy was applied once and reported; it does not
say what to do when the remedy fails, so nothing further was invented.

**Consequence: condition (iii) of the decision rule — "keeps held-out FDR within the caps" — is NOT
satisfied for L.** Everything below uses the recalibrated floors, and `thema_L` is 2,815 themes.

Two things also worth recording. First, this was found by Aviyah, not by my check: my gate read a
key named `fdr` where `confirm()` writes `heldout_fdr`, got `0.0` for every stratum from the
default, and printed "worst stratum 0.0 — PASS". The same class of bug as `engine_gates.py` reading
G1 from the manifest. The gate now **raises** on an unrecognised shape instead of defaulting, since
a silent default there approves a build that failed its own check. Second, the earlier claim in this
file that "the floors transferred to L unchanged" was wrong and is withdrawn: **they did not
transfer.** L is the first universe where the stored floors fail, after Reactome-only (0.00126) and
BioLORD (0.00067) in Test R, where they held.

## Test T — the text placement works; the themes it needs mostly do not exist

Every summary was removed before every build, so no theme ever saw one. T1 places each by its own
description vector alone, with no size information.

| arm | band | n | text | gene ceiling | random floor | best possible | share > 0.5 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| L | 3–10 | 856 | 0.154 | 0.293 | 0.001 | 0.258 | 0.067 |
| L | 11–50 | 280 | 0.105 | 0.224 | 0.002 | 0.210 | 0.029 |
| L | 51–200 | 48 | 0.044 | 0.191 | 0.002 | 0.122 | 0.000 |
| (a) | 3–10 | 856 | 0.200 | 0.356 | 0.000 | 0.333 | 0.118 |
| (b) | 3–10 | 856 | 0.133 | 0.288 | 0.002 | 0.286 | 0.068 |

Read the two right-hand columns together. The **random floor is 0.001** and text placement reaches
0.154 — about 150× chance. But **"best possible" is 0.258**: the highest Jaccard *any* theme in the
build reaches, so the build itself caps the score and text gets 60% of the way there. The binding
constraint is the clustering, not the placement.

**T2 separates the two, and this is the result worth keeping.** Restricted to summaries that some
theme *does* recreate at Jaccard > 0.5:

| arm | recoverable summaries | text ranks it #1 | in the top 5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| L | 101 of 1,185 | **64.4%** | **86.1%** |
| (a) restricted | 204 of 1,185 | 52.9% | 84.8% |
| (b) HiDeF k4/800 | 102 of 1,185 | 62.7% | 89.2% |

**When a theme exists that recreates a held-out summary, text alone finds it first about 64% of the
time and in the top five about 86%** — from the description, with no names, no gene counts, no size
information and no curated input, choosing among 2,815 candidates. That is a far better
placement signal than the direction pilot produced (62.5% on a *two-way* choice against a 70% bar).

The failure is upstream: only **101 of 1,185** summaries (8.5%) have any theme that recreates them.

## What this means

The leaves experiment set out to replace a text-direction problem with a clustering problem: remove
the summaries, and see whether the themes rebuild them. **They do not.** 8.5% of summaries have a
theme that recreates them, and removing the summaries first makes recall *worse* than leaving them
in and hiding them afterwards — most likely because the summaries' own descriptions were anchoring
the regions they summarise, so taking them out removed scaffolding rather than freeing the leaves to
organise themselves.

The hypothesis in the spec — *"from the atomic pathways of four databases, text alone merges
cross-database duplicates and recovers the curated summary structure"* — is **not supported**. L
also fails the FDR condition, so it loses on all three of the declared rule's conditions.

But Test T found something the primary test was not looking for. **Given a theme that does
correspond to a summary, text alone picks it out of 2,815 candidates 64% of the time at rank 1 and
86% in the top 5.** The placement machinery works; what is missing is themes at the right
granularity to place onto. That reframes the open problem — away from "can text tell general from
specific", which the direction pilot answered badly and which Test T suggests was the wrong
question, and towards **"can the clustering be made to produce mid-level themes at all"**. The
reviewer's §2c says why it currently does not: general pathways cluster with general pathways, and
there is no middle level for text to aim at.
