# Are L1's large themes unions of smaller themes? 24 Oct 2026

Prompt 62, designed by Aviyah and the reviewer. **Evaluation only: no rule is applied and nothing is
removed.** Every measure is exact and checked against an explicit loop over sets on a random sample
of 200 targets (seed 20261024); both passes came back identical on all four comparisons.

The set is the **90%-each-way** themes found in **at least 10 runs** -- Aviyah's merge-rule decision
of 24 Oct, and a **provisional working bar**, not a calibrated one. That is 52,001 accepted themes,
of which **7,958 have 25 or more pathways** and are measured.

**The short answer: the mid-sized themes are unions of unrelated blocks and the giants are not.**
Once a theme's own near-copies are set aside, 57-62% of accepted themes between 25 and 199 pathways
are covered 90% by three mutually distant picks -- but that falls to 16% at 400-799, **4.1% at
800-1,599 and 2.8% at 1600+**. The four giants need more than three pieces: three distant blocks
cover only 76-84% of each.

**But the broad domains really are inside the giants, as big chunks.** `set_3` (2,271) contains
`set_6` (1,143 metabolism) *exactly* -- J = 1.000, its single largest distinct block, 50.1% of it.
`set_4` (2,267) contains `set_5` (1,491 core cell machinery) at J = 0.766 and `set_7` (867 immunity)
at J = 0.966, and **those two together cover 86.7% of it.** So the blind reading was right about
what the giants are made of and wrong only about how few pieces it takes.

## What was measured, and the one thing that had to be added

For every accepted theme `T` of 25 or more pathways:

* **inner themes** of `T` are accepted themes smaller than `T` with at least 90% of themselves
  inside `T`;
* **single cover** is the largest share of `T` one inner theme covers;
* **greedy cover** repeatedly takes the inner theme covering the most still-uncovered pathways of
  `T`, recording coverage after one, two and three picks and `J(pick 1, pick 2)`;
* the **two-block pattern** is coverage of at least 90% after two picks with those picks below
  `J = 0.5` of each other; the **three-block pattern** is at least 90% after three picks with every
  pair of the three below `J = 0.5`.

Greedy ties are broken by larger overlap with the target, then larger inner theme, then lower theme
index -- declared so the pick sequence is reproducible rather than an argmax's internal detail.

**Inner themes are defined by containment, so a theme's own near-copies qualify.** An accepted theme
one pathway smaller and otherwise identical is 100% inside `T` and covers 93% of it alone. That is
what the declared measure says, and it is why the declared first pick is a near-variant of the target
in essentially every band: **median single cover 0.929 across all 7,958 targets**, with median
`J(pick 1, target)` far above 0.5. Under that measure "two-block" mostly means "pick 1 was a variant
covering 93% and pick 2 was a small distant remainder" -- it answers *can one theme cover T* rather
than *is T two unrelated blocks*.

So both passes are reported: the **declared** one, and an **added** second pass that drops inner
themes within `J < 0.5` of the target -- the same 0.5 the block patterns already use. The added pass
is the one that answers the question in the prompt's WHY paragraph. **Nothing is removed from any
build by either pass.**

## What this says

1. **The unions question has a size-dependent answer.** Between 25 and 199 pathways, 57-62% of
   accepted themes are three unrelated blocks and about 26% are two. Above 400, both patterns
   essentially vanish. Any rule aimed at unions would act almost entirely on the middle of the
   distribution, not on the giants that motivated the question.
2. **The giants are made of domains, but of more than three pieces.** Three disjoint blocks cover
   76-84% of each of the seven sets and never 90%. The blind reading of "two or three domains glued
   together" is right in substance -- `set_4` is 86.7% `set_5` plus `set_7` -- and optimistic about
   the count.
3. **Containment is the wrong relation for a same-theme test and the right one for a
   parent-child test.** `set_6` sits inside `set_3` at J = 1.000 and they are separate themes: the
   90%-each-way rule cannot see it because `set_6` is only 50% of `set_3`. That is not a bug in the
   merge rule -- a half is not the same theme as its whole -- it is a statement that **the
   relationship L1 is missing here is hierarchy, not duplication**, which is what level 2 is for.
4. **Half is the recurring number.** Excluding variants, the largest distinct piece of an accepted
   theme is 0.485 to 0.501 of it in every band from 25 to 1,599. Whatever is generating these
   themes is generating them in halves.
