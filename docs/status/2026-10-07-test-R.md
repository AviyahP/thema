## A rewrite that mattered

My first `score_side` looped over (grouping, run) pairs and called the LCA on one-element arrays.
At the real scale — **73,483 groupings × 200 runs ≈ 5 million walks**, each costing a few hundred
NumPy calls on arrays of length one — that would have taken hours. It was killed after the pool
printed and rewritten **run-major and vectorised**: one pass per run, every qualifying grouping in a
single batch, seven vectorised LCA folds and a short vectorised climb. **Scoring went from
hours-projected to 17 seconds.**

Because that is exactly the kind of rewrite that silently changes an answer, there is now an
**equivalence test**: a deliberately naive reference implementation — one grouping, one run, one walk
at a time — on a fixture with mixed grouping sizes and real 80% draws, asserted to match the fast
version on `h_full`, `g_loo`, `g_life` and the run counts, including the NaN pattern. It does.

## Two discrepancies in the transfer brief, flagged not resolved

**1. `embeddings_biolord.npy` does not cover the 10,770.** The file is **(1854, 768)** — the v0.1/v0.2
universe — so transfer (ii), "the full 10,770 with the existing embeddings_biolord.npy", cannot be
built from the existing file. BioLORD-2023 *is* wired into `embed.py` and *is* already in the local
HuggingFace cache, so the matrix can be produced locally with no API spend, and Aviyah put "the
BioLORD trees" in scope. **So I encode the v0.3 universe's descriptions with BioLORD into a new
file** and use that, rather than skipping the setting or silently substituting the 1,854 matrix.
Worth knowing: BioLORD's effective limit is 128 tokens (`embed.py:45`) against MedCPT's longer
window, so these descriptions are truncated for it — which makes transfer (ii) a *harder* test than
a like-for-like re-embed, not an easier one.

**2. The Reactome-only universe is 2,836 rows, not 2,883.** `pathways.tsv` holds 2,883 reactome
rows, but the **embedded** v0.3 universe holds **2,836** of them — 47 are absent from the universe
the build actually uses. Test R uses the 2,836 that exist, because the alternative is a universe
whose rows have no embedding. The 80% sampling rule then draws ⌈0.8 × 2,836⌉ = **2,269** per run.

## Interpretation decisions, recorded before computing

The spec leaves three things open. Each is resolved the narrow way and stated here so no result
later rests on an unexamined reading.

1. **The scored population is the POOL**, i.e. deduplicated candidate groupings with support ≥ 0.33
   and size 3–9 — not the completed families. The spec says "every candidate grouping g … (the
   existing fixed-cut families, real and scrambled)", and those are the same population seen at two
   stages: a family is the completion of a grouping. `copies` — which run found what — exists only
   at the pool stage, and `h_full` needs it. **Consequence: item 1's FDR is a ratio of groupings,
   where Test F's was a ratio of families, so the two sets of numbers are not interchangeable.**
   Both floors and gap are computed on the same unit here, so the comparison inside Test R is sound.
2. **"The Ward height at which g's cluster forms"** is the height of the full-dendrogram node equal
   to the matched copy in that run. The copy is a recorded cluster, and recorded clusters map back to
   dendrogram nodes exactly, because the recording rule is reproducible.
3. **"The height of g's parent"** is the immediate merge that consumes g's node in the full
   dendrogram, not the nearest *recorded* ancestor. The recorded set omits nodes below `min_size`
   and above the cap, and a lifetime measured to the nearest surviving ancestor would be a different
   quantity.

# THE HEADLINE: G_loo is INVERTED. The gap is real, and it belongs to the noise.

Calibrated on seeds 3001–3003, pool groupings of size 3–9 at support ≥ 0.33.

## G_loo separates real from scrambled — backwards

| size | real median G_loo | scrambled median G_loo | real n | null n | **G_loo AUROC** | G_life AUROC | support AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.993 | **1.602** | 8,641 | 1,163 | **0.0901** | 0.9290 | 0.7629 |
| 4 | 1.050 | **1.682** | 13,793 | 2,254 | **0.0715** | 0.9703 | 0.8523 |
| 5 | 1.078 | **1.692** | 13,405 | 140 | **0.0948** | 0.9792 | 0.8995 |
| 6 | 1.086 | **1.672** | 11,792 | 9 | **0.1116** | 0.9905 | 0.8826 |
| 7–9 | 1.102 | — | 25,852 | **0** | — | — | — |

**An AUROC of 0.07–0.11 is not a weak score, it is a strongly anti-predictive one.** Scrambled
groupings have *larger* leave-one-out gaps than real ones, at every size, by about 60%. The
hypothesis was that a real group would have to climb further before an outsider arrived. It is the
scrambled groups that climb further.

## Why, and the mechanism is clean

The gap logic has the geometry right and the population backwards. **A real small theme sits in a
crowded neighbourhood** — pathways about the same biology are numerous and close, so when one member
is dropped the nearest outsider is right there, and the gap is small. **A scrambled grouping that
survives support ≥ 0.33 survives precisely because it is isolated**: in a structureless embedding the
only things that recur are points in sparse regions with nothing near them, so the next merge is far
away and the gap is large.

So the score does measure isolation. Isolation is just not what distinguishes real biology here — it
distinguishes *sparse* from *dense*, and real small themes are the dense ones.

## What that costs in real themes kept

**A correction to my own first framing.** I first compared the gap gate against the **fixed 0.33
cut** and reported that it keeps 70.9%. Criterion (b) compares it against the **floors**, and on
that comparison the per-size gap gate keeps **52,098 against the floors' 55,055 — 94.6%**. The gate
is far closer to viable than the fixed-cut comparison made it look, and the real damage is
concentrated rather than general: see item 2 below.

Thresholds solved on 3001–3003 at ratio ≤ 0.01: t[3]=1.7419, t[4]=1.7592, t[5]=0.8903,
t[6]=0.9377, t[7–9]=0.9357; single t = 1.7110.

| size | fixed 0.33 keeps | gap, per-size t | gap, single t |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 8,641 | **128** (1.5%) | 131 |
| 4 | 13,793 | **921** (6.7%) | 952 |
| 5 | 13,405 | 13,404 | 1,246 |
| 6 | 11,792 | 11,791 | 1,267 |
| 7–9 | 25,852 | 25,851 | 3,258 |
| **total** | **73,483** | **52,095 (70.9%)** | **6,854 (9.3%)** |

The per-size version looks survivable only because of an artefact: at sizes 5–9 there is almost no
scrambled grouping to begin with (140, 9 and **zero**), so the ratio target is met at a cut *below*
the real median and the threshold keeps everything. Where there is real noise to suppress — sizes 3
and 4 — it keeps **1.5% and 6.7%**. The single-t version keeps **9.3% overall**.

**Criterion (b) — "keeps at least as many real 3–9 themes as the floors" — fails by more than an
order of magnitude, for both versions.** Under the verdict declared before any of this was computed,
that settles it.

## The side-finding, which is the useful part

**G_life — the classic lifetime, parent height over own height — separates real from scrambled at
AUROC 0.929 to 0.991**, in the expected direction and better than support alone (0.763–0.900) at
every size. It was specified as the secondary score and it is the one that works. Test R's real
contribution is therefore not the leave-one-out idea but the observation that a cheap per-group
lifetime carries most of what the scramble floors are buying.

## What Test R contributes

The leave-one-out idea does not work, and it fails in an informative direction: it measures
isolation, and real small themes are the *dense* ones. **G_life, the classic lifetime, separates real
from scrambled at AUROC 0.929–0.991** — better than support alone at every size — and that is the
result worth following up, which is what arm K now does.
