# The DAG of L1's recurring themes, strict containment. 24 Oct 2026

Prompt 63, Aviyah's design. Exploratory; **nothing is adopted and no membership is changed** -- no
inheritance, no chain collapse, no merging. `A` is a parent of `B` when every member of `B` is in
`A` and `A` is larger, reduced to immediate parents only.

The set is the **90%-each-way** themes found in **at least 10 runs** -- a **provisional** working
bar, not a calibrated one -- with members at inclusion >= 0.5. That is 52,001 themes. The ancestor
relation and the reduction are exact and **verified against explicit set loops on 300 random themes
(seed 20261024): 300/300 ancestor sets and 300/300 immediate-parent sets identical.**

**The headline is a three-pathway failure.** Prompt 62 found that `set_6` (1,143, metabolism) sits
inside `set_3` (2,271) *exactly*, and that `set_4` holds `set_5` and `set_7`. In the strict DAG
**all seven large7 sets are roots with no parent at all.** `set_6`'s theme is **99.56% inside**
`set_3`'s theme -- **5 pathways** keep it a root -- and `set_7`'s theme is **99.65% inside**
`set_4`'s -- **3 pathways**. Across the whole DAG, **218 of the 594 roots are one pathway away from
having a parent**, and 418 of 594 would gain one at a 90% tolerance.

So strict containment does not express the hierarchy that is visibly there. That is a fact about the
relation, not about the themes.

Four things to take from this.

1. **Removing the 3-5 themes removes 25 roots and nothing else** (594 -> 569), and that is not a
   measurement so much as an identity: a theme's parents are strictly larger, so a theme of 6 or
   more can never have a 3-5 parent, and dropping them cannot change its root status. The 25 are
   small isolated themes, and 18 themes have no parent *and* no child.
2. **The top is all roots by construction of the relation.** 100% of the 1600+ themes and 98% of
   800-1599 are roots -- they are near-copies of one another, so none contains another, so none can
   be anyone's child. The 594 roots are not 594 top-level concepts; they are mostly the variant
   cloud at the top plus a scatter of tiny isolated themes.
3. **91.16% of themes have two or more parents**, against 63.3% in O50s. With 5.5 million ancestor
   pairs reduced to 388,287 edges, a theme has a median of 4 children and sits under several
   parents: this is a dense lattice, not a tree.
4. **17,433 themes have a member set identical to some other theme** -- 52,001 themes carry only
   34,568 distinct member sets. The merge rule compares **cores**, so two themes with different
   cores can end up with the same membership and never be merged. That is the duplication prompt 62
   measured, seen from a different angle, and it is the single biggest cleanup available.

pathways, mean 775, from 50 up to 2,394. It is a far bigger reading job than the earlier batches --
228,630 member lines against the 12,846 of `large7/` -- and worth knowing before starting it. The id-to-theme mapping, with size and runs found, is in
`read_roots/KEY_DO_NOT_SHARE_L1_roots.json`, written once and not read again; it is excluded from
the commit script.

## What this says

1. **Strict containment is the wrong relation at this tolerance, and the margin is tiny.** Three
   pathways separate `set_7` from being a child of `set_4`; five separate `set_6` from `set_3`. The
   hierarchy prompt 62 saw is real and the DAG cannot see it. Some tolerance is needed, and the
   measurement says a one-pathway tolerance already changes 218 roots.
2. **But a 90% tolerance is too much, in a specific way.** It would empty the top completely -- 100%
   of 400-799 roots, 99% of 800-1599, 83% of 1600+ would gain parents -- which does not build a
   hierarchy so much as decide that the giants nest inside each other. The right tolerance is
   somewhere below 90% and probably absolute (one or two pathways) rather than proportional, because
   the near-misses are overwhelmingly off-by-one.
3. **The duplication has to go first.** 17,433 of 52,001 themes repeat another theme's membership
   exactly, 91% of themes have multiple parents, and 18.5% of edges barely narrow anything. Any
   hierarchy built on this population inherits a lattice of variants. Deduplicating on **membership**
   -- not on cores, which is what the merge rule compares -- is the obvious missing step, and prompt
   61's and 62's results both point at it.
4. **The 594 roots are not 594 concepts.** 132 of them are themes of 800+ that are near-copies of
   one another, and 25 are tiny isolated themes. The seven distinct large sets are the real top, and
   they are flat.
