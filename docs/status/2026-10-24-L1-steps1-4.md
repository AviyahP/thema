# Level 1 of the core-recurrence design, steps 1-4. 24 Oct 2026

Prompt 61, Aviyah's design of 23-24 Oct. Exploratory: **no gate, no consensus, no repair, no edges,
nothing adopted.** Three sides were built identically -- the real one and prompt 60's two scramble
seeds -- and **every step is verified against an explicit brute-force loop** (seed 20261024), plus
five invariants checked over every theme rather than a sample.

**The headline is not what the design expected.** The prompt says large structure should come later
from level 2, "not from this step". It comes from this step: **the ten largest themes found in 10 or
more runs of 200 hold 2,325 to 2,412 pathways**, at 10 to 63 runs. Arm K's ceiling of 59 pathways at
a 20-run bar was not a fact about the resampling -- it was a fact about arm K's stricter match.
Letting the copy miss 30% of the drawn members changes the answer completely.

**But the large end is about seven objects, not hundreds.** The 237 themes of 800+ members found in
10 or more runs collapse to **7 mutually distinct sets** at Jaccard 0.5 (2,412, 2,381, 2,271, 2,267,
1,491, 1,143, 867) whose union is all 5,539 pathways. They are the dendrogram's top-level splits,
recovered many times over as near-variants. The same ratio holds all the way down: in every band
from 25 up, the recurrent themes collapse to about 5% of their count.

**And the small end is noise.** At a 5-run bar the two scramble seeds produce *more* 3-5 themes than
the real side does (143%), and 41% as many 6-11. From 12 pathways up the scramble is empty. So this
design's usable floor is a **size** floor near 12, not a support floor -- which is the opposite shape
from the floors O50s calibrates.

### The match, and why it is 70% on each side

For a seed `C` and a run `r`: `r` can judge `C` if at least 3 of `C`'s members were drawn. `D` is the
**smallest cluster of `r`'s tree holding at least 70% of `C`'s drawn members**, and `r` finds `C` if
in addition `C` makes up at least 70% of `D`. Prompt 58 showed why Jaccard on the union cannot do
this: two clusters that are each 70% the same core score only about 0.54 together, so no useful
Jaccard threshold sees them as copies.

`D` is unique whenever it exists, and that is a property of the tree rather than a tie-break: two
clusters each holding 70% of the same set cannot be disjoint, so all the qualifying clusters are
nested and form a chain with a single smallest element.

**The size window is exact, not a heuristic.** `D` holds at least `0.7k` of the `k` drawn members, so
`|D| >= 0.7k`; and if `C` is to make up 70% of `D` then `|D| <= k / 0.7`. The smallest qualifying
cluster therefore lies in `[0.7k, 1.43k]` whenever the pair is found at all -- and if nothing in that
window qualifies, the true smallest qualifying cluster is bigger than `k / 0.7` and fails the second
test anyway. That is what turns 200 runs x 270,570 seeds of ancestor walking into a handful of
chunked sparse products per run: **65 seconds for the whole real side.**

**`C`'s own runs are counted exactly as O50s counts them**: a run in which the seed appears as a
cluster is eligible and found by definition, with the copy being the seed itself
(`recurrent.score`, `src/thema/ontology/recurrent.py:739`). This is written out because the two rules
can disagree -- the smallest cluster holding 70% of `C` inside `C`'s own run can be a child of `C` --
and the origin convention wins, as it does in O50s.

### How much distinct structure is there, really (an extra, not a prompt section)

The counts above say the themes overlap; they do not say how many distinct objects are left. Taking
the themes found in 10 or more runs, largest first, and keeping each only if it is below J = 0.5
against everything already kept -- a greedy count **within each band**, so a 199 and a 201 are never
compared -- gives:

| band | themes found in >= 10 runs | distinct at J < 0.5 | share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 25-49 | 6,332 | 340 | 5.4% |
| 50-99 | 2,157 | 117 | 5.4% |
| 100-199 | 876 | 49 | 5.6% |
| 200-399 | 415 | 20 | 4.8% |
| 400-799 | 269 | 10 | 3.7% |
| 800+ | 237 | **7** | 3.0% |

**About 5% at every size.** The large themes are not hundreds of giants; they are seven, each
recovered dozens of times at slightly different boundaries, and their union is the whole universe of
5,539 pathways. Whatever the merge rule at J >= 0.9 is doing, it is not collapsing variants: it
leaves roughly twenty near-copies standing for every distinct object.

## What this says

1. **The match is the whole story.** The only difference between arm K and L1 is that L1 lets the
   copy miss 30% of the drawn members. Arm K found nothing above 59 pathways at a 20-run bar; L1
   finds 40 themes of 1,600+ at the same bar. Any conclusion of the form "there is no large recurrent
   structure in this resampling" belongs to a match rule, not to the data.
2. **The large structure that exists is thin.** Seven distinct objects above 800 members, covering
   the universe between them. That is a top-level partition recurring, which is real but is not an
   ontology.
3. **A support bar cannot clean the small end and a size floor can.** 3-5 themes are indistinguishable
   from scrambled embeddings up to a 10-run bar; 12-24 themes are clean at a 5-run bar. This is the
   first result on this universe that argues for gating on size rather than on support.
4. **The merge at J >= 0.9 on cores is too tight to be the deduplicator.** It collapsed 261,984
   cored groups to 137,008 themes, and about twenty near-copies per distinct object survive (34 in the 800+
   band). Whatever
   level 2 does, it inherits a population that is 95% variants.
5. **Half of O50s falls out of four exact steps with no statistics.** 45.8% at J >= 0.9. That makes
   the gate, consensus and repair stages answerable questions rather than assumptions: they are worth
   what they add on top of this, and that is now measurable.
