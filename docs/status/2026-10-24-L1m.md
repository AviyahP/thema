# L1m: the merge on members, repeated until stable. 24 Oct 2026

Prompt 64, Aviyah's design and decision. Exploratory; **nothing adopted.** The 90%-each-way test is
applied to **members** (inclusion >= 0.5) instead of cores, and the pass repeats until nothing
changes. **Prompt 61's steps 1 and 2 are unchanged** -- they are recomputed here rather than read
back, because `l1_build` persisted the theme-level arrays but not step 1's copies, its judge matrix
or the group-level inclusions; the printed counts match prompt 61's note line for line (10,525,135
copies, 261,984 groups with a core).

**It works, and the duplication is gone completely.** Seven passes converge geometrically --
138,045 merges, then 3,481, 253, 22, 2, 1, 0 -- to **120,180 themes with zero identical member sets**
(L1 had 18,918 over its 131,373 themes, and 17,433 among the 52,001 it accepted). Each pathway now
sits in **102.6** themes rather than 210.7, and non-nested near-copies fall to **5.9 per theme** --
**below O50s's 15.7** for the first time in this line of work.

**The giants are still flat, and now that result is robust.** All seven `large7` sets match an L1m
theme at J >= 0.91 and all seven are still roots with no parent -- but where prompt 63 found a 90%
tolerance would reparent 83% of the 1600+ roots, **a one- or two-pathway tolerance reparents none of
them.** The seven are genuinely not nested in one another; prompt 63's near-misses at the top were an
artefact of the variant cloud, not evidence of hierarchy.

**The giants do not grow by merging; they shrink or stand still.** Seven of the ten are unchanged

traced through the seed chain, so each row follows one theme rather than an arbitrary constituent.

The full-pass replay runs only where all-pairs set arithmetic is feasible, so on the real side it is
reported as skipped rather than quietly omitted. **It does run on the 6-run smoke, where it
reproduces the vectorised greedy's assignment array exactly** (11,023 themes, every theme assigned to
the same seed), which is what makes the greedy itself -- not just the adjacency it walks -- a checked
claim.

## What this says

1. **Comparing members instead of cores fixes what prompt 63 diagnosed, completely.** Zero identical
   member sets, asserted and guaranteed by the fixed point; themes per pathway halved; redundancy per
   theme below O50s's for the first time; 85% fewer near-misses; the top of the DAG down 63%.
2. **The repetition matters for the guarantee, not the count.** Pass 1 does 98% of the merging. But
   without passes 2-7 the result is not a fixed point, and re-pooling after a merge changes members,
   so a single pass leaves pairs that now pass the test. The cost is 6 extra passes at ~2 s each.
3. **The giants are flat, and that is now a finding rather than an artefact.** Seven distinct large
   sets, all roots, and an absolute one- or two-pathway tolerance reparents none of them. Whatever
   relates the giants to each other, it is not containment at any tolerance worth using.
4. **The new problem is the small end.** 3-5 themes grew by 3,200 and 61.7% of them are matched by
   scrambled embeddings at the 10-run bar. Re-pooling pushes members below the 0.5 cut, and the band
   that grows is the only band noise can reach. A size floor near 12 -- which prompt 61 already
   argued for -- would remove 24,930 of the 120,180 themes and the entire noise-dominated region.
5. **A hierarchy still has to come from somewhere other than strict containment.** 617 roots, 82%
   multi-parent, 161,869 edges, and 71% of the 12-24 roots one pathway from a parent. The tolerance
   question is now well posed: absolute, small, and applied below the giants.
