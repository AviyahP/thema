## 22:55Z — §2's byte-identity test, run EARLY, caught a 60% inflation bug

I ran the §2 byte-identity check before any ablation number existed, against v0.3's cached material
rather than against my own expectations. It failed, and the cause was mine.

| | families |
|---|---|
| my first pipeline, all switches on | **259,924** |
| v0.3 | **161,852** |

**The stage ORDER was backwards.** v0.3 completes every grouping *first*, drops those whose completed
membership falls below `min_size`, and only then runs seed absorption over the **completed** bitsets
(`families(list(completed), completed, pool, …)`). My version absorbed raw groupings and completed
afterwards. Absorption on raw groupings merges far less, because raw groupings are more varied than
their completions, so 60% more families survived.

**Had I not checked, every ablation in §1 would have been measured against a baseline that was not
v0.3**, and the size-cap ablation in particular would have looked enormous: cap-off gave 260,063
families against cap-on's 259,924 — a difference of 139 — while I was about to attribute the gap to
the cap.

Fixed, and re-validated: **161,852 families, byte-identical to v0.3 as a multiset of
(members, support).** The wrongly-ordered side is kept under `ablate/superseded_wrong_order/` rather
than deleted.

A second, smaller fault found alongside: `families()` must be passed `settings`, or it falls back off
the fast set-similarity join and the stage takes 400 s instead of 58 s. That cost one wasted 11-minute
side before I noticed.

# 2026-10-08 — §2 and §3. v0.4 is built, proved, and as good as v0.3

## §2 — the pipeline, and the proof that it is a simplification

`src/thema/ontology/v04.py`, 400 lines. The whole method is: match groupings across 200 Ward runs
on 80% subsamples, complete each to the union of its matched copies, absorb into families at
θ = 0.70, keep what clears a per-size support floor, merge near-duplicates at the same θ, and stack
by strict containment. Three knobs: 0.8, θ = 0.70, and the floors.

The four removed stages are **absent, not switchable**, and each carries its ablation number in the
module docstring. The three kept stages carry theirs in `STAGES`, as data, so a report can print the
justification without reading the file.

**The byte-identity proof ran before any v0.4 number.** `v04_run.py --all-stages-on --verify`
restores every removed stage, cuts the real side with v0.3's cap, gates with v0.3's own floors read
from the frozen manifest, and compares:

```
material against the frozen side: 161,852 candidates against 161,852  membership IDENTICAL
identical  nodes.tsv    identical  members.tsv    identical  edges.tsv    identical  unplaced.tsv
PASSED. With every stage on, v0.4 reproduces the frozen build exactly.
```

The comparison is at two levels on purpose. Reading the frozen cache and only comparing the built
tables would have left the matching, completion and absorption in `v04.py` unproven — which is most
of it, and the part the stage-order bug was in. So the side is **recomputed** through `v04.material`
and checked against the cache first; if that fails the build comparison is skipped as meaningless.

**Two real bugs this caught**, both of which would have invalidated every §3 number:

1. **The size cap came out 3,124 instead of 3,125.** I wrote the share as a truncated decimal and
   took `int()` where v0.3 takes `round()` of `2 * 469 / 3233`. The constant is now that same exact
   rational, and `tests/ontology/test_v04.py` asserts it against `cut_trees.CAP_SHARE`.
2. **The inclusion column was written as 1.0 for every member.** v0.3 records what completion
   actually produced (0.9574, 0.4795, …). The first comparison came back with every node, edge and
   unplaced row identical and **40,501 of 98,599 member rows differing** — which is exactly the
   signature of losing a column while keeping the membership. `Material` now carries `selections`,
   `build()` refuses material without them rather than silently writing 1.0, and the node's
   inclusion is the accepted candidate's own vote, as v0.3 writes it.

The same 1.0 flaw was in `v04_build.py` and therefore in all seven §1 ablation builds. **It does not
affect any §1 decision**: recall, precision, FDR and stability all read member *sets*, never the
inclusion column. The §1 table stands as reported.

**Calibrated once.** Held-out FDR **0.00237** on scrambles 4001–4002, against the 0.01 cap — one
check, as declared. Floors: 3-3 0.79, 4-4 0.91, 5-5 0.3687, 6-6 0.33, 7-9 0.33, 10+ 0.33. Stored at
`data/ontology/v0.4/calibration/floors.json`. The scramble sides are reused from the `no_size_cap`
ablation because the computation is provably the same one — side material reads only completion,
absorption and the cap, and v0.4 differs from that ablation only in STRAY and containment, neither
of which enters it. The manifest records the reuse.

Output: **`data/ontology/v0.4/thema_10770/`**, 6,104 themes.

## What was removed, and is v0.4 as good as v0.3?

Four stages are gone. The **size cap** (29.0133%, with its own reference script) removed about two
candidates per tree and moved held-out FDR by 0.00004. **TOL** was already inert — it is read only on
a branch no build takes. **STRAY** (0.10) let consensus inherit members and changed nothing
measurable. **Partial containment at 0.9** produced a build identical to strict containment on all
four statistics, which is what "report-only" should always have meant. Three stages stayed because
removing any one of them broke something: completion (stability 0.855 → 0.713), greedy absorption
(FDR doubles), per-size floors (four independent failures at once, including the FDR cap).

**Yes, v0.4 is as good as v0.3.** On the test half, read once, no cell differs beyond the margin in
either direction, on either GO closure; FDR is marginally better (0.00237 against 0.00241);
stability is 0.3 points lower, inside tolerance; the DAG is still 100% gene-monotone. It has 140
fewer themes, 41 fewer roots, more multi-parent nodes (35.2% against 29.5%) and 120 more effectively
unplaced pathways — the last is the one place v0.4 is visibly different, and it comes from dropping
the size cap, which had been pulling a few large candidates back into the DAG.

What v0.4 does **not** fix is the placement failure the reviewer identified: curated parents are
still not put above their children (46.1% among untied pairs, against 46.2% for v0.3). That is not a
stage that could be ablated away — see
[`2026-10-08-monotonicity.md`](2026-10-08-monotonicity.md) and the leaves experiment it motivates.
