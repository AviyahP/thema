# The eight giants are overlapping bags, not areas. 9 Oct 2026

Declared 9 Oct before any result. **Report only — nothing was rebuilt.** Everything is read from the
cached `thema_L_repair` build and the cached real trees; the only thing written is one HTML file.

**The finding is a clean separation.** The eight giant roots are **gene-incoherent** — coherence
0.68× to 4.04× against a size-matched null, one of them *below* random — while **their children are
excellent**: median 23.9×, and **99% of them clear 3×**. And the giants are not distinct from each
other: across the eight there are 2,609 child slots but only **1,376 distinct children**, so each
child sits under 1.9 giants on average, and for three of the giants **80–89% of their children are
shared with another giant**.

So the top of this build is eight overlapping bags drawn around the same ~1,376 children. Reading
them as eight biological areas is reading structure that is not there.

**And removing them does not reveal a top level** — it reveals that there isn't one. 7,460 of the
8,785 surviving themes had *every* root above them among the giants, so deleting the eight turns
**124 roots into 905**.

## Part 2 — the build with the eight giants removed

A counterfactual on the cached build, not a new build. A theme is counted as newly top-level only if
**every** root above it was a giant, so nothing is dropped silently.

| | `thema_L_repair` | **without the 8 giants** |
|---|---:|---:|
| themes | 8,793 | 8,785 |
| **roots** | **124** | **905** |
| themes whose every root was a giant | — | **7,460** |
| largest remaining roots | 2,614, 2,586, 2,527, 2,261 | **1,405, 838, 826, 800, 400, 373, 294, 211, 201, 151** |
| **max children** | **533** | **219** |
| median children | 2 | 2 |
| **multi-parent** | **62.5%** | **51.8%** |
| leaves reaching a 51–300 theme | 2,554 (46.0%) | 2,554 (46.0%) |
| leaves reaching a 301–1000 theme | 2,483 | 2,483 |
| leaves reaching only 11–50 | 522 | 522 |

**Removing the giants improves every shape number and makes the top level worse.** Max children
533 → 219, multi-parent 62.5% → 51.8%, and leaf placement is untouched because the giants were never
any leaf's smallest container. But **roots go 124 → 905**, because 7,460 themes — 85% of the build —
had nothing above them except giants.

That is the answer to the question the counterfactual was asked: **the giants are not hiding a top
level, they are substituting for one.** They are the only thing above most of the build, and they do
that job badly — incoherent, mutually overlapping, and sharing 80%+ of their children.

The largest remaining root is `n01268` at 1,405 members (the 1,000+ theme that was never a root),
then 838, 826, 800 — so without the giants the top is still four near-thousand-member themes, then a
long tail. There is no clean 20–40-area level waiting underneath.

## Part 3 — the browsable export

`data/ontology/v0.4-leaves/thema_L_repair/browse.html`, 3.4 MB, self-contained: no fetch, no
network, no external reference (`file://` blocks fetch, so the data is embedded). Roots → children →
members, expandable, with **size, support, support at 0.5 and coherence on every node**.

`scripts/export_browse.py` was **not reused.** It renders the named 1,850-pathway v0.2 build, expects
theme names from the namer — which has never been run on a universe-L build — and computes neither
support-at-0.5 nor coherence. `scripts/export_browse_leaves.py` is a separate exporter that keeps its
principles: one file, nothing fetched, and every number labelled with the universe it came from.

What the page states on its face, because a reader would otherwise be misled:

- the universe is the **5,559 atomic** pathways, not the 10,770, so no figure on it is comparable
  with a 10,770 number;
- themes are **unnamed**, so each shows the member names nearest its centroid — those are members,
  not a label;
- **support** is at 0.70 and **s@0.5** beside it, because the gap is the most informative thing
  about a large theme;
- **coherence** is against a size-matched null, and anything under 3× is flagged as not
  distinguishable from a random set of its size;
- the **eight giant roots are flagged as such**, with the reason: incoherent (0.7–4.0×) while their
  children are not (median 23.9×), and sharing most of their children with each other.

Verified after writing: the embedded payload parses (8,793 nodes, 5,559 names, 124 roots, 8 giants,
166,642 member entries), no placeholder is left unfilled, and the page makes no external reference.

## What this means

The repair of 9 Oct removed stale merges and chain near-copies, and the wide roots survived it. This
says why: **the giants are not duplicates of each other in the sense either fix tests for.** Their
pairwise Jaccard is below 0.70 — they are genuinely different sets — while still drawing 80–89% of
their children from the same pool. No merge rule at 0.70 can see that, and raising the threshold
would start merging real themes.

Three things follow, none of them a proposal:

1. **The mid-level layer exists and is good.** 107 distinct themes of 51–300 members under the
   giants, median coherence 23.9×, 99% above 3×. Whatever is wrong with this build is not that the
   middle is missing or noisy.
2. **What is missing is a level above the middle.** The eight giants occupy that slot without
   earning it: incoherent, overlapping, and the sole parent of 85% of the build. Deleting them leaves
   905 roots, so nothing is waiting behind them.
3. **The support gap runs through all of it.** Children at coherence 20–30× carry support 0.26–0.93
   at 0.5 and far less at 0.70; `n07992` is at 0.030 and 1.000. The strict Jaccard is not measuring
   whether these groups recur, it is measuring whether they recur *identically*, and a 2,000-member
   group never does.

Nothing is frozen. **Aviyah decides.**
