All six pairs score below κ 0.04, and two of them share no gene at all. Neither the names nor the gene lists connect these pathways, so no gene-overlap tool groups them.

Because they barely overlap, each one is a separate observation. Four of them landing on the same theme is stronger support than any one of them alone.

Every one of the second pathway’s 167 genes is already in the first. It brings nothing of its own — given the first, it could not have come out any other way.

The third shares only 21 genes with the first and carries 57 that GO does not have. Its enrichment is real added support. The names give you no way to tell which is which.

THEMA groups pathways by their thematic meaning, not by the genes they share — so related biology scattered across seemingly unrelated pathways can be pulled together into meaningful clusters.

The ontology is built over 10,817 pathways over four databases (Reactome, Gene Ontology, MSigDB Hallmark and the Blood Transcriptional Modules) before any experiment meets it. Your enrichment is laid over that pre-computed hierarchy, not used to build it.

Every cluster carries the union of its pathways’ gene lists. Given a gene list and the universe you measured, enrichment can therefore be computed at every level of the hierarchy — and what a parent theme adds beyond its enriched members can be tested directly.

THEMA is a research tool under active development. The hierarchy is rebuilt as its source databases are updated, and each build is versioned. Pathway descriptions are generated by a language model from curated source text and gene membership, then checked by an automated verification pass; they are not a substitute for the curated descriptions they were written from, and both are shown side by side for every pathway. github.com/AviyahP/thema
